# Luma40

## Overview

Silver / Kailh White Rain Switch

Always wanted to try a true ortholinear keyboard. Might not be the most ergonomic, but aesthetics are nice.

Very compact. Aluminum case makes it feel very premium and hefty. There’s an optional strap for carrying. 

![Luma40, lights off.](DSCF3255.jpg)

Translucent keycaps let the light shine through very nicely. However, even though the switches are premium, the keycaps make it feel a bit cheap.
![RGB lights on!](DSCF3266.jpg)

No USB dongle, just Bluetooth or wired.
![View of the back showing the USB-C connector and wire/bluetooth toggle, as well as the mount point for the optional strap.](DSCF3258.jpg)

Thin rubber pads at the bottom, very sturdy.

![Underside of the keyboard.](DSCF3270.jpg)

No stabilizers on the space bar, so can feel a bit “bindy” depending on how you hit it.

It’d be a nice tv/couch keyboard if you have a computer connected to your tv.

## Teardown

With a small keyboard like this, layers are important. Default keymap is ok.

![Keycaps removed.](DSCF3416.jpg)

Five screws later and the PCB is free from the case.

![PCB removed, exposing a foam panel at the bottom of the case.](DSCF3424.jpg)

![1450mAh battery is nice and thin, glued securely to the case.](DSCF3433.jpg)

Microcontroller is unknown MILLER N25NOV2017.

![Close up of the PCB.](DSCF3435.jpg)

Removing switches was actually quite difficult, would not recommend. Tolerances on the plate appear to be too tight for the side tabs on the Choc switch. Was only able to get this corner one out by lifting the corner of the plate and wiggling it out.

![Switch removed, exposing thin gasket between the plate and PCB.](DSCF3440.jpg)


I would probably switch out the keycaps, but otherwise it’s nice. Fun to type on such a small, solid board. Not a huge fan of the transparent keycaps, and not as thocky sounding as the others, but feels nice. Honestly grew on me the more I used it; first impressions were not the best but got better, mainly because of how the keycaps felt.

# Hack 70

## Overview

 White / EPOMAKER Creamy Jade Switch

 - One of their newest keyboards
 - Ortholinear. Nice because it fits quite a few keys in a small package, taking up less desk space and minimizing the distance from keyboard to mouse
 - Uses QMK, so fully programmable. Only via though, so no support for combos 
 - Legends are nice, print quality is good and crisp, font is modern
 - Sounds really good, gasket mounted
 - Excellent build quality
 - Really like how they chose split spacebar
 - Split spacebar allows using one of them as a dedicated layer key, so something like SpaceFN can be used without input lag
 - I personally like using the layer for arrow keys on hjkl, and hotkeys like browser forward/back, tab forward/back, change workspace, etc


![Hack70 Keyboard.](DSCF3224.jpg)

Interesting choice of having backspace and enter keys be 2U on a mostly-ortholinear board. Aesthetically it looks nice, but it adds some layout quirks which I’ll get to later.

## Layout

Good variety of keys, but the right side has some interesting placements. 
- \` is between \' and `ENTER`, so easy to accidentally hit tilde instead of ENTER since it's one column over
- Most annoying thing is how the spacebar is not lined up with the keys, instead of 2U and 3U it’s not ortholinear
- Bothers me because standard ortho layouts have the space right below the n, but now it’s shifted ever so slightly so it was throwing off my typing
- I was typing and wondering why I was making so many mistakes, this was why
- Would have preferred two 2U spacebars unde VB and NM, then this would open an additional key below ,
- Everything else about the keyboard is great, but this layout issue bugs me and may bug others who are already used to ortholinear layouts
- Probably took regular shift key (1.25U?) and split spacebar (1.75U?) for these dual spacebars
- Requires tucking the thumb slightly in, which is less than ideal
- Might have preferred if the backspace and enter were 1U instead so it’s like a true ortho layout, then the keyboard could be 1 less column and would also remove the issue of enter being one key too far.

Not true ortholinear, not saying that as a purist but because it requires some adjustment for typing since the spacebar is not in the typical place of right below the N. Requires tucking the thumb slightly more, which threw off my typing.

In comparison with the Luma40 above where the (single) spacebar is between the B and N (as is typical).

# Split70

## Overview

 White Blue / Wisteria Linear Switch V2
 
Volume knob and macro keys on left are nice and feel premium. Sounds decent. Keycap legends are not the most crisp though.

Interface between the two is also magnetic, which is nice, since keeping halves positioned relative to each other is convenient.

Does take up more space though since it’s row staggered, notably the inner gap between the two halves.

![](DSCF3294.jpg)

![](DSCF3301.jpg)

Backside. No adjustable feet if that’s your thing, but nice sturdy rubber pads to keep it still.

## Layout
One of the main issues with row-staggered splits is the B key, since many people (including myself) hit it with the right index finger, but with splits like the Split 65 the B is on the left side instead, making for a potentially frustrating experience as you're getting acclimated to the keyboard. So nice that there's B on both sides.

Right shift is a single 1U key instead, which may or may not be an issue if you use it often. Otherwise it's a pretty standard layout.

# Final Thoughts

- Impressed overall, good value for money especially the Hack70. But spacebar design leaves a bit of a stain on what would be a pure keyboard, same with enter and backspace keys
- Having been in the ergo mech scene for so long, forgot how nice regular mech keyboards can sound, and they’ve gotten to a much better price point than the availability of decent mech keyboards from 5-10 years ago
- The enclosures on the Split70 and Hack70 not having exposed screws for disassembly make it much more difficult to repair
