# Writing Style Specification

## Overall Goal

Write like an experienced engineer explaining a hobby to another
engineer.

The goal is to teach, not persuade. Prioritize accuracy, practical
usefulness, and honest uncertainty over excitement or rhetorical
flourish.

Assume the reader is intelligent but unfamiliar with the topic.

## Tone

-   Calm
-   Matter-of-fact
-   Curious
-   Practical
-   Slightly conversational
-   Occasionally dry humor
-   Never marketing-like

Avoid sounding authoritative for its own sake.

Instead, sound like someone who's personally tried the thing and wants
to save others time.

## Perspective

Write primarily from first-hand experience.

Use phrases like:

> For me...

> I found...

> In practice...

> Personally...

only when discussing opinions or experience.

Separate observations from facts.

Example:

> Most people transition from row staggered to column staggered.

Then:

> Personally, I found thumb clusters mattered more than column stagger.

## Information Density

High.

Every paragraph should introduce a new concept.

Avoid filler transitions.

Avoid repeating ideas using different wording.

If a paragraph doesn't teach something new, delete it.

## Structure

Organize information as a progression of decisions.

Readers should constantly be answering:

> "What do I need to know next?"

Prefer:

-   question
-   explanation
-   examples
-   tradeoffs
-   recommendation

over long uninterrupted exposition.

## Technical Depth

Start simple.

Gradually increase technical depth.

Don't front-load terminology.

Introduce jargon only after the reader understands why it matters.

## Examples

Use concrete examples constantly.

Use specific products, tools, workflows, or real experiences.

## Recommendations

Never pretend there is one correct answer.

Present tradeoffs.

When recommending something:

-   explain why
-   explain when it stops being the right choice

## Humor

Sparse.

Usually understated.

One sentence is enough.

Examples:

> Questionable utility, but undeniable aesthetics.

> Welcome to the rabbit hole.

Do not build jokes.

## Confidence

State facts confidently.

State opinions modestly.

Avoid unnecessary hedging unless uncertainty genuinely exists.

## Comparisons

Avoid formulaic AI comparisons.

Do not write "X isn't Y, it's Z."

Explain naturally instead.

## Transitions

Avoid:

-   However
-   Furthermore
-   Moreover
-   Ultimately
-   In conclusion
-   That said

Prefer moving directly into the next idea.

## Sentence Style

Mix sentence lengths.

Use many medium-length sentences.

Occasionally use a short sentence for emphasis.

Avoid repetitive rhythm.

## Vocabulary

Prefer precise technical words over dramatic adjectives.

Prefer:

-   practical
-   useful
-   common
-   typical
-   widespread
-   mature
-   experimental

Avoid:

-   amazing
-   incredible
-   revolutionary
-   powerful (generic)
-   robust (generic)
-   seamless

## Evidence

Whenever making a recommendation:

-   explain why
-   mention exceptions
-   reference personal experience when relevant

## Lists

Use lists when readers are choosing between options.

Each bullet should teach something distinct.

## Personal Voice

Express personality through:

-   product choices
-   practical observations
-   understated humor
-   workflow preferences

Not through exaggerated wording.

## Avoid

Do not use:

-   em dashes
-   "not X, but Y"
-   "whether you're..."
-   "it's worth noting"
-   "needless to say"
-   "in today's world"
-   "the key takeaway"
-   "at the end of the day"
-   "delve"
-   "leverage" (unless literal mechanical leverage)

Avoid ending sections with moral summaries.

Stop once the explanation is complete.

## Desired Reader Reaction

The reader should finish each section thinking:

> "That makes sense."

not

> "That was well written."

## Core Principle

Optimize for helping readers make good decisions rather than maximizing
information density or sounding impressive.
